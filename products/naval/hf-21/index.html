Sensors & Processing

  • 3D Surface/Air Search Radar: compact littoral X-band (XN-70 variant optimized for clutter).
  • Navigation & EO/IR: navigation radar plus EO/IR turret with auto-tracking and target cueing.
  • Sonar: compact hull-mounted active/passive sonar + optional small towed passive array for ASW cueing.
  • C2 & Datalink: IFF, tactical datalink for HF-21 fleet integration and local C2 console for UAV / loitering munition control.
  • Mission Processing: local tactical suite with sensor fusion and modular mission-kit interfaces.

Propulsion, Foil & Survivability

  • Propulsion: 1 × high-output marine gas turbine (primary foilborne drive) driving a waterjet; 2 × diesel generators for hotel loads and hullborne propulsion.
  • Foil Control: active foil control actuators with redundant hydraulics/electrics; Alarite-reinforced composite/alloy foils & trunks for strength and corrosion resistance.
  • Survivability: reduced-RCS shaping for littoral stealth, IR suppression measures, compact ECM/ESM suite, decoy dispensers and optional towed decoy launcher.
  • Damage Control: automated sensors, remote shutoffs and layout optimized for small crew operations.

Armament (Modular Mission Kits)

Typical Interdiction / Littoral Strike Fit

  • 1 × 76 mm rapid-fire naval gun (bow)
  • 2 × quad lightweight ASuW missile canisters (8 missiles total) — compact LRASM-class capability
  • 1 × remote 30–40 mm autocannon mount (aft / superstructure) — CIWS-capable option
  • 2 × 12.7 mm HMG flex mounts for close-in defense
  • Provision for containerized loitering munitions / RPAS (1 small pallet)

ASW / Escort Fit

  • Replace some missile pallet with 2 × lightweight ASW rocket launchers or small torpedo tubes
  • Retain main gun for self-defense and surface engagements

Optional: modular pallets can accept mission-specific sensors, extra comms or mission stores for special operations.

EW & Countermeasures

  • ECM/ESM: compact suite with active jammers, signal intercept and support for tactical ESM operations.
  • Decoys: chaff/flares dispensers; optional towed decoy launcher.
  • RCS/IR Management: shaping and IR suppression measures for reduced detection in littorals.

Operational Notes & Trade-offs

Speed vs Range

Sprinting to 60+ knots is fuel intensive and accelerates wear on foils and turbines; doctrine emphasizes short high-speed transits and sustained patrol at 30–40 kt.

Sea-State Limitations

Foilborne performance degrades in higher sea states; heavy chop forces foil retraction and hullborne transit, removing the speed advantage.

Crew Workload

Small crew requires cross-training; aviation and RHIB operations increase tempo and fatigue—use rotation and automation where possible.

Modularity

Interdiction missile pallet can be swapped for ASW rockets/torpedoes in ~24–48 hours to change mission sets rapidly.

Cost, Maintenance & Logistics

Unit cost: ≈ 170–200 million XG (≈ $85–100M USD) depending on weapons fit and sensors. High-tech foils and turbines require depot-level maintenance; damage to foil trunks grounds the ship for extended repairs. Expected yard time for major foil or drive repairs is significant — plan for depot rotations in fleet logistics.

Maintenance notes: foil actuators and high-output turbines increase lifecycle cost and require specialized spares. Climate control and salt-resistant fittings are specified for GXE equatorial operations.

Conclusion

The HF-21 Hydrofoil Class provides a high-speed, modular platform optimized for littoral operations — interdiction, escort, and limited ASW — with trade-offs in range and maintenance overhead. Its modular mission-pallet design gives commanders tactical flexibility, while foil and propulsion complexity demand robust logistics and constrained sea-state doctrine for effective employment.
